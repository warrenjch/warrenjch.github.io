## 1. A Cockroach's Life

When we arrived at the hostel we had booked in Bangkok, there was an unwelcome guest waiting on the window ledge near the upper floor. A large cockroach, fiddling its legs and antennae and looking right at me. There was cockroach spray, which I'd never used before because we had cockroach traps and repellant at home in Singapore, and I never had to face one before, not even in the army (where I saw them, but just left them alone if they weren't in my bunk).

It was surprisingly easy to kill it with the spray, but on the first spray it actually jumped towards me, so I quickly emptied the whole clip at it (sprayed it 4 times). During the 3rd spray it twitched and spasmed until it fell with a thud onto the top of a counter downstairs, where it lay twitching and dying for another minute until the 4th spray guaranteed that it couldn't have been more dead, with all its legs in the air, frozen in a twisted convulsion.

In that moment, I thought to myself: I'm glad that between me and the cockroach, I was the more advanced being.

Later I looked up whether cockroaches feel pain from bug spray. Here's what Google's AI had to say about it:

> Cockroaches do not experience pain the way humans do, because they lack a complex brain and emotional processing system. However, insecticide sprays contain neurotoxins that trigger physical distress, severe nervous system disruption, and prolonged paralysis before they die. While roaches possess basic pain receptors (nociceptors) to detect harmful stimuli, scientists debate whether they feel conscious, emotional suffering.

I guess we will never know; and neither would the cockroach know or even come close to comprehending, up until the second it died, why all of a sudden it was sprayed with some mysterious thing that liquefied its muscles and burnt its insides, and why the larger, more advanced being in front of it wanted it dead so badly.

## 3. A World Full Of Ramanujans

I think it's time to take the idea of an [<u>alien mind</u>](https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/){: .page-link target="_blank"} far more literally. It's 2026 and we found a bunch of intergalactic comets with alien runes containing unbelievable amounts of knowledge. The world's leading scientists get to decoding the knowledge, and just the bits that we have managed to decipher with our limited knowledge of their language has solved many of the most complex problems that we've faced in our quest to understand the universe.

Do you think that under this alternative framing, society's best and brightest would: a) bemoan the loss of their jobs as the sole proprietors of new knowledge, or b) immediately summon as many smart people as they can to see how much more we can decipher and understand, so that we can improve human civilization with this newfound knowledge, or at the very least understand the universe and our existence better? Do you think that they would continue to treat papers and citations as the source of legitimacy or status within the academic circle?

I envision that in this alternative world, we will have a million Ramanujans, who dream of fantastic concepts as they sleep, dreaming through the alien runes pulsating with knowledge. If we wanted to, we could discover ways to bring more people towards the point where they could understand these alien concepts. Perhaps there is danger in these runes so that we should take care in deciphering them, or perhaps some organizations and individuals may want to control the alien knowledge for their own interests, but I know for certain: the one thing the academic community should not be doing is to sigh, "But in our *current system*, we have been rendered useless!" Well, regardless of whatever else happens in the world, this alien knowledge will be there waiting for pioneers to interpret it and add them to the great tower of human knowledge - and if not by our smartest, then by whom?
